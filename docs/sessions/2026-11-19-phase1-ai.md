# P1-11 — Headless AI and paired tournament

The three-layer player selects science or domination with integer utilities and a six-point switching margin. Its operational planner budgets expansion and infrastructure over the remaining research horizon, chooses settler sites and decomposes research and building prerequisites. The tactical search retains four joint plans and uses simulation forecasts for legal orders and damage. ADR-0010 records the controller and referee contracts. The published P1-10 AI crate contained only a bootstrap marker, so there was no earlier released player to enter.

## Independent review and validation

Review found unequal access to banked research, policies and district sites in the baseline, and a founding order that could survive a lost movement conflict. All controllers now share legal research chaining, policy unlocks and settlement/district candidates. The referee suppresses dependent settlement orders after a lost move. Tests cover these cases through the command registry.

Development used seeds 0–31. The earlier evaluation on seeds 10000–10031 was already visible when review requested changes. We set aside seeds 20000–20031 before testing the revised players and froze the policies before running those games. Both players started with the same resources, exchanged seats on each generated map and had 500 turns to win.

All 128 games ended in science victories. Against greedy, the candidate won both games on 31 maps and split one pair (one-sided paired sign-test p = 4.66 × 10⁻¹⁰). Forty-three otherwise legal orders were invalidated or suppressed by simultaneous conflicts. All 128 outcomes and terminal hashes matched on repeat and with eight requested workers (at most two seats planning concurrently). The [tournament log](2026-11-19-phase1-ai-tournament.txt) includes reproduction commands and individual greedy results.

The release benchmark measured 1,000 samples per layer on a contested seat. Strategic, operational and tactical p95 were 0.2 µs, 40.4 µs and 61.8 µs respectively on the local Windows GNU machine.
